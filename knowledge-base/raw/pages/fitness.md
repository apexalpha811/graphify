# Fitness

Training and nutrition principles grounded in current evidence. Based on 2026 ACSM guidelines and established strength/hypertrophy research.

## Training Principles

### Strength
- **Progressive overload**: the non-negotiable. Add weight, reps, or density over time
- **Intensity**: train at or near failure (1-3 RIR = reps in reserve) for best stimulus
- **Frequency**: 2-4x per week per muscle group. More frequency = more practice = faster skill and strength gain
- **Specificity**: to get stronger at a movement, do that movement
- **2026 ACSM guidelines**: resistance training for healthy adults based on 137 systematic reviews, 30,000+ participants. Wide rep ranges work provided effort is sufficient

### Hypertrophy (Muscle Building)
- **Rep range**: muscle grows across 5-30+ reps. Pick loads you can push near failure
- **Volume**: sets per muscle per week is the key driver. 10-20 working sets/week per muscle is evidence-supported range
- **Effort**: proximity to failure matters more than the specific rep range
- **Mind-muscle connection**: produces measurably better hypertrophy for isolation work
- **Exercise variety**: hit muscles from different angles over time

### Nutrition for Body Composition
- **Protein**: 0.7-1g per pound of bodyweight per day. Non-negotiable for muscle retention and growth
- **Caloric surplus for muscle gain**: 200-500 calories above maintenance. Modest surplus = lean gains, less fat
- **Caloric deficit for fat loss**: 300-500 calories below maintenance. Preserve protein intake to retain muscle
- **Whole foods baseline**: outperforms every diet trend. Minimally processed foods, adequate fiber, micronutrients
- **Meal timing**: less important than total daily intake. Pre/post workout protein helpful but not critical

### Recovery
- **Sleep**: 7-9 hours. Most important recovery tool. Growth hormone and testosterone peak during sleep
- **Deload**: every 4-8 weeks reduce volume by 40-50% for one week. Prevents accumulative fatigue masking progress
- **Stress management**: cortisol impairs recovery and muscle protein synthesis
- **Active recovery**: walking, light movement on off days accelerates recovery vs total rest

## Common Splits
| Split | Best For |
|-------|----------|
| Full body 3x/week | Beginners, limited schedule |
| Upper/Lower 4x/week | Intermediate, balanced development |
| Push/Pull/Legs 6x/week | Advanced, high volume |
| Body part split | Advanced, specific weak point focus |

## Key Metrics to Track
- Weekly bodyweight (7-day average smooths fluctuations)
- Strength on key lifts (squat, deadlift, bench, overhead press, row)
- Progress photos every 4 weeks (visual changes lag the scale)
- Sleep quality and HRV if using a wearable

## Tools
- Training logs: Strong app, WHOOP, Garmin
- Nutrition: Cronometer (most accurate database), MacroFactor (adaptive calorie targets)
- Programming: RP Strength app for hypertrophy, Stronger by Science templates for powerlifting
